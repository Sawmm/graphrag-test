## Section 2 — Training Dataset
*(Annex IV §2 — development process; Article 10(2)–(4))*

### 2.1 Design choices  `Art. 10(2)(a)`
> Why was this dataset chosen for this purpose? Selection criteria, intended coverage, scope.

BiomedCLIP is pretrained on PMC-15M, described as "a large-scale parallel image-text dataset generated by [the BiomedCLIP] data pipeline for biomedical vision-language processing." The dataset was chosen because it contains 15 million figure-caption pairs extracted from biomedical research articles in PubMed Central, covering a diverse range of biomedical image types including microscopy, radiography, histology, and more. The large scale and biomedical diversity were the stated rationale. A data pipeline was developed and released at https://github.com/microsoft/BiomedCLIP_data_pipeline to automatically download and process articles from the PubMed Central Open Access dataset.

### 2.2 Provenance  `Art. 10(2)(b)`
> Origin of the data — where it came from, how collected, institutions involved, legal/consent basis.

PMC-15M was generated from the PubMed Central (PMC) Open Access dataset. The data pipeline automatically downloads and processes articles from PMC Open Access. The dataset contains figure-caption pairs extracted from biomedical research articles in PubMed Central. The data pipeline is publicly released at https://github.com/microsoft/BiomedCLIP_data_pipeline. No information about legal basis, consent, IRB approval, or institutional review is provided in the model card. The use of open-access PMC content is implied but not explicitly stated.

### 2.3 Preprocessing  `Art. 10(2)(c)`
> Cleaning, filtering, annotation, labelling, augmentation, and other transformations applied.

The model card states that the BiomedCLIP Data Pipeline "automatically downloads and processes a set of articles from the PubMed Central Open Access dataset" to generate figure-caption pairs. No further detail on cleaning, filtering, figure extraction, caption normalization, image resizing, or other preprocessing steps is provided in the model card. The paper is referenced for additional details. The model uses PubMedBERT as the text encoder and Vision Transformer (ViT-Base/16) as the image encoder; contrastive learning with a context length of 256 tokens is used.

### 2.4 Assumptions  `Art. 10(2)(d)`
> What does the dataset claim to represent? Stated limitations of that representation.

PMC-15M is described as covering "a diverse range of biomedical image types, such as microscopy, radiography, histology, and more" extracted from biomedical research articles. The implicit assumption is that PMC Open Access articles are representative of biomedical image-text content broadly. No explicit discussion of representational assumptions, limitations, or caveats about the PMC source population is provided in the model card.

### 2.5 Suitability  `Art. 10(2)(e)`
> Size, coverage, and any explicit fitness-for-purpose assessment.

The training dataset contains 15 million figure-caption pairs. Coverage spans diverse biomedical image types: microscopy, radiography, histology, and more. The scale (15M pairs) and diversity are implicitly presented as justifying suitability for a general biomedical vision-language foundation model.
